# Concordance of Characters

## Principal Characters

**Ernesto Klee**
Former detective, protagonist. Pronounces his name "Clay" (everyone else says it to rhyme with "free"). Wardrobe full of thermals. Addicted to buses, burgers (Billy's Burgers, always the same order), and four-shot coffees. Ex-wife has left but is unaccounted for. Suffers from 4:44am dread. Now working as a live-in security guard in a deconsecrated Unitarian chapel in Kings Norton, Birmingham. Has a self-taught memory technique: at crime scenes (and cafés) he silently lists and re-lists what he sees, talking to himself in words (he is not a visual thinker, despite colleagues assuming he had a photographic memory). Picked up the habit from a burgled magician's library of memory books, and honed it as a child memorising the prizes on the conveyor belt of the TV show "Well, Then You Have a Go!" (WTYHAG). Left-handed. Reads Hornblower / naval historical fiction in the canteen because "it helps him think"; prefers walking and chatting to formal questioning ("not a big fan of asking questions"), which makes him unpopular with the corrupt faction who want him gone.
_Chapters: 1, 1.5, 2–21 (+ drafts: Roof)_

**Jackie Spanner**
Klee's former stakeout partner and closest friend. Tall, giant size-twelve feet, chronically untidy, always eating prawn cocktail crisps. Obsessed with catching corrupt police. Loved horror movies and prawns. Found dead on his icy patio – barefoot, in his wife's dressing gown, with a shotgun. Klee is certain he was murdered. Had texted Klee hours before his death: "The call's coming from inside the house."
_Chapters: 1, 1.5, 2, 4, 5, 7, 14_

**Yannick Pausing**
Klee's former sergeant (corrects Klee at the end: he was actually promoted to inspector just before he resigned). Still in contact with Klee via covert YouTube cypher — Klee posts an encoded message on an agreed video and Pausing calls him back. Arranged the Birmingham chapel job as cover for Klee. Klee calls him "Yan" (also rendered "Jan" once in Ch 19); he calls Klee "Ernie." The corgi Maggie is actually his ex-partner Astrid's (see below), left with him. By Ch 21 it emerges that the whole chapel job traces back to Astrid, with whom he has a shared history — they nearly went to jail together in Colombia, and he'd told Klee he'd have "nothing more to do with" her.
_Chapters: 2, 4, 6, 13, 14, 19, 21_

**Astrid**
Pausing's on-again-off-again partner (the "former partner" who left the corgi Maggie behind) and the hidden mover behind the chapel job. Swiss — she is the "German" neighbour Mrs Ting complains about, cooking currywurst and fondue at 45/next to the chapel. Believed the dead "Boht" might be Dobby Brand and that the Collaigne-Schotts gold was in the chapel; her hired men ("bozos") broke in searching and never found it. Nearly landed Pausing in jail in Colombia once. Surfaces in person in Ch 21, sitting in the middle of the maze with Klee and Pausing, demanding "Where's the gold, Ernie?"
_Chapters: 19 (offstage), 21_

## The Chapel Household

**Ulysses Boht**
The old man who owned and lived in the chapel at 47 Pachelbel Road. Died approximately two weeks before Klee's arrival. Filled the chapel with antiques, cigarette cards, paintings, and a Victorian Berber tent. Behind the chapel: a Sunday school room turned labyrinthine library. The freezer in the cellar contains a frozen body. By Chapter 14, it is established that "Boht doesn't exist" – the name is a false identity. Per Mrs Ting (Ch 17), Boht bought the chapel and everything in it from Didier D'Aubeilles in a hurried sale ~six months after D'Aubeilles took it on – D'Aubeilles "had to go abroad in a hurry" and left everything, even his clothes. Boht told Mrs Ting to keep cleaning but to stop going into the cellar, claiming he'd seen a rat and locked it; the cellar (roughly the size of the chapel itself) has been padlocked ever since. Deliberately styled himself to resemble the 40-year-old press photo of Dobby Brand (big black eyebrows, heavy black-framed glasses) — but where Dobby had a mole, Boht had an obvious scar, "as if he was saying 'look! It's Dobby! I had my mole removed!'" The Boht draft floats the theory that "Boht" was in fact **two people** taking turns (hence Mrs Ting finding him forgetful, paying her twice, his English sometimes good and sometimes terrible) — "Boht A" (found dead in the freezer) and "Boht B," with Terpsichore proposed as Boht B and thus a ~50/50 chance of being the killer.
_Chapters: 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 12, 13, 14, 17, 18 (+ drafts: Boht, Roof)_

**Mrs Ting**
Neighbour at 45a Pachelbel Road. Elderly, fierce, convinced the people next door are Germans who are always cooking German curries. Widowed (husband Jimmy died 1995 – "an awful man and I'm glad he's dead"). Was Boht's cleaner; cleans and does laundry at the chapel. Younger-looking than Klee expects when he finally sees her properly. Cleaned for D'Aubeilles before Boht (kept his business card in her notebook). Knows the chapel's history – the leaking, painted roof that forced the Unitarians to auction it, and the existence (and location) of the padlocked cellar. Fond of the old Minister, Gordon Thorpe.
_Chapters: 3, 7, 8, 9, 11, 17, 18_

**Lillian Jones (née Ting) / Lilly**
Mrs Ting's daughter, lives with her at 45a. A lot of red hair, pale skin, freckles. Works shifts (nurse? Not specified). Married name Jones – says the name is "about the only good thing I got from my husband." Meets Klee in a coffee shop in town. Was a little girl when the chapel was still in use; barely remembers it. Her coffee with Klee in Chapter 17 is interrupted and taken over by Mrs Ting.
_Chapters: 3, 5, 7, 17_

**Jimmy Ting**
Mrs Ting's late husband. Died 1995. Mrs Ting is not grief-stricken.
_Chapter: 9_

## The Criminal Case

**Darren Oliver Brand ("Dobby" Brand)**
Master criminal. Mastermind of the Cadfield Farm heist (early 1990s) and the Collaigne-Schotts bullion robbery (October 1994 – 247 kilos of gold, current value ~£25 million). Nicknamed "the General" for military-style planning. Signature: no violence, hospital anaesthetics to incapacitate guards, meticulous money-laundering plans. Disappeared after the 1994 job. Was never caught. A body is found in the freezer in the chapel cellar (reclining on packets of frozen peas and fish fingers), apparently dead since Christmas 1994 and killed by a blow to the head (possibly a frozen leg of lamb). Whether this body IS Dobby is deliberately left ambiguous by Ch 19–21: it has a scar where Dobby's mole should be and only "a bit" resembles him — "like maybe Dobby would have looked if he'd had plastic surgery" — so it may be an impostor made up to look like Dobby (whose wrong fingerprints would then protect the real story). The gold itself is hidden in plain sight throughout the chapel — the "art deco bronzes," fire extinguishers, and mesh-weighting bricks are all solid gold. Klee's key deduction: there is **too much gold** (variously "twice" and "three times" more than the 247kg Collaigne-Schotts haul), implying loot from more than one source.
_Chapters: 13, 14, 18, 19, 21 (+ drafts: Boht)_

**Jonathan Terpsichore ("Mr Turps")**
Presents himself as Boht's nephew, a banker in New York. Hired Klee via Pausing to guard the chapel. Frail-looking on video call, sandy hair, blue eyes, always in business attire. By Chapter 14 it is confirmed he does not exist – someone has assumed this name as a false identity. The real person with this name is not the client. The Boht draft proposes he is "Boht B" (the surviving half of a two-man Boht) and therefore stands a ~50/50 chance of being the man who killed the body in the freezer; his hired men are among the several parties who broke in searching for the gold.
_Chapters: 10, 12, 14 (+ drafts: Boht)_

**Didier D'Aubeilles**
A famous collector – "jewellery, books and antiques, gold a speciality" (per his business card, kept by Mrs Ting). Owned the Kings Norton chapel for about six months after buying it at the Unitarians' auction, before selling the whole place – contents, furniture and even his clothes – to Boht in a hurried sale, having to "go abroad in a hurry." Klee recognises him as quite famous (especially for collecting gold) the moment Mrs Ting names him. The likely original source of the chapel's collection. The Roof draft reframes his acquisition of the chapel and hints he may be Dobby Brand himself: he bought it not at auction but via a private cash purchase (claiming the money came from selling a farm in South Africa), laundered through the solicitor Grafton; Pastor Johnson, shown Dobby's photo, allows "it could have been" him and remembers "that mole."
_Chapter: 17 (+ drafts: Roof, Boht)_

**Aimee Finch**
A journalist. Four foot eleven, seven stone. Known for being able to look after herself (had poked men in the eye, kneed one in the balls). Found beaten to death in the car park of the Isambard Kingdom Brunel pub in Muswell Hill – her skull smashed with an unusual spanner, bound with string and glue, very hard to get prints from. Her murder on 17 July 1996 was Klee's first day on the job. Became the most expensive, most investigated, and still unsolved murder investigation in Metropolitan Police history. The case Jackie Spanner refused to stop investigating – which is likely what got him killed.
_Chapters: 1, 7_

## The Criminal Underworld

**Bernie Gersten ("Big G")**
One of Klee's closest criminal contacts. Operated clubs in Birmingham (a decision he regrets). Sits in a very dark office. A man to be careful with – there was once a moment when it looked like he might throw Klee in a canal ("That was all a misunderstanding. They'd sorted it out. They'd laughed about it later. Well, Bernie had laughed."). Recommended Preston Vanz for security work.
_Chapters: 11_

**Preston Vanz**
Security and surveillance specialist. Dreadlocks. Techno wizard. Was in high-security prison because he kept breaking out of open prison and hacked the prison CCTV. Son of Dalton Vanz. Mrs Ting takes an immediate shine to him and plies him with tea and cake. Pausing's plan at the end involves using Preston as bait — Klee is told to call him and take the car out of the garage. Installed Klee's chapel security (multiple layered camera/lock systems that even he says only a "mission-impossible level" hacker could take down at once). The Cubit draft casts him in a darker light: in a phone argument he and his father Dalton are shown secretly hunting the same gold, and Preston's own security is now what's keeping them out — Dalton's separately-hired "local morons" are the amateurs who spooked Klee by breaking in the back door.
_Chapters: 11, 14 (+ drafts: Cubit, Roof)_

**Dalton Vanz**
Preston's father. Now retired. Was the security man Bernie Gersten used to use. In the Cubit draft he is revealed to be after the chapel gold himself, pushing his son Preston to break in; it was Dalton who hired the "local morons" whose clumsy break-in alerted Klee. Divorced — his wife left him over an affair with "Debbie."
_Chapters: 11 (+ drafts: Cubit)_

**Henry Jayne**
Celebrity antiques expert and Klee's go-to for valuing the chapel's contents (earlier Klee avoided calling him because "Henry would tell everybody where he was"). A reformed thief: as a young assistant curator at Shelaghbrightdown (pronounced "Sheldon") Hall he stole high-priced antiques and was caught trying to fence them in an antique shop owned by his victim, Lord Barry Gatling – himself a thief who hated being stolen from. Jailed, then "taken from the country jail" to become the nation's favourite antiques expert on the TV show *X Marks the Spot* (part antiques show, part treasure hunt). Knew little about antiques at first, but after a prison conversation with the "Crickley Hill killer" (a non-random, business-motivated murderer who dismembered four people across Gloucestershire) he devoted himself to study; his specialism is porcelain and he has written several books. Has a "villain's sense" of whether an object is pukka or moody. Aspiring vegan; takes leaf tea with cow's milk ("palaeolithic" if available) because almond milk "just sits at the bottom of the cup and sulks." Calls Klee "Ernesto(s)." Drives up from out of town to inspect the chapel: identifies the naked-lady lamp as a fake (but worth "considerably more" than the £5,000 a bronze original would fetch) and spots a privately printed copy of Harrison Corner's *Pissing Blue Steam* in the library maze.
_Chapters: 5, 16_

**Div**
Colour-blind car dealer with cars on a forecourt. Former car thief who was a rising star in that community before his wife Nav straightened him out. Gave Klee the lime-green muscle car ("Key Lime Pie" – it has flames down the side). The car's actual owner is in the Whittington Hospital, deep in a coma after going through his windscreen.
_Chapters: 2, 6_

**Nav**
Div's wife. The reason Div is not in jail. Said the lime-green car was "bright."
_Chapter: 2_

**Banks**
Mentioned in passing: "What happened to Klee? Did Banks and his boys finally catch up with him?" Appears to be someone with authority or muscle who is looking for Klee.
_Chapter: 5_

**Mohammed Hossain**
Owner of the Crystal Palace Tandoori and Grill. Delivered Jackie Spanner's final meal (king prawn biriyani) himself on the night Jackie was killed. One of the few people who can give a timeline for Jackie's last hours.
_Chapter: 1_

## Minor / Background Characters

**Mrs Petaloudes**
Klee's neighbour on his old street. Feeds birds compulsively — originally in the park, but was banned and received an ASBO for it. Her son-in-law negotiated a workaround: feeding grain on the street rather than duck bread in the park. Wears all black including headscarf, drags a black shopping cart. Attended by a retinue of pigeons, enormous seagulls, and ducks who have apparently followed her from the park. Her habit of spreading grain across car bonnets triggers the explosion that ends Chapter 1.5.
_Chapter: 1.5_

**Elaine Sketchley**
Someone Klee slept with. Described as a "bad idea."
_Chapter: 12_

**Maggie**
The corgi. Left with Pausing by his previous partner — revealed in Ch 21 to be **Astrid**. Reappears with Astrid in the maze in Ch 21 (Klee greets her by name). The Ch 15 café "easter egg" (a corgi-shaped, grey-blue-spotted dog with pale grey-blue eyes near Klee) now resolves as Astrid, not just Pausing, lurking near Klee.
_Chapters: 4, 14, 21 (Ch 15 easter egg)_

> _Easter egg (Ch 15):_ in Klee's local café, a thirtysomething woman with short hair and no wedding ring has two dogs – one a poodle-ish cross, the other "the shape of a corgi, but with grey-blue spots on a white background" and "pale, grey-blue eyes." Klee doesn't remark on it, but the description pointedly echoes Maggie, hinting at a Pausing connection lurking near Klee without his noticing.

**Kevin ("Jewish Kevin")**
The friend whose live-in security guard business Pausing is covering. Rediscovered his Jewish heritage after his divorce, came into money (in the wrong order for the ex-wife), went to the south of France and met someone. Away indefinitely.
_Chapter: 4_

**Vanessa**
A veteran stripper. Her observation that tips were dropping off – because her regulars were going less often and spending less – was the insight behind the "stripper theory" of predicting recessions. Had a speciality act with counter-rotating tassels. Told young economist Bernard she was hanging up her pasties.
_Chapter: 13_

**Bernard**
The young economist who frequented strip clubs, befriended Vanessa, and used her declining tips as a recession indicator. Wrote a book called *Stripper Theory*. Won a Nobel Prize.
_Chapter: 13_

**Gordon Thorpe**
The Minister of the Unitarian chapel before it was deconsecrated. Mrs Ting thinks him "a lovely man"; Lilly's late father couldn't stand him ("He didn't like anybody! Miserable sod!"). Worried the painted plaster roof would collapse mid-hymn ("Sing Hosannah") and, faced with the cost of repair, oversaw the auctioning-off of the chapel. **Note:** the Roof draft recasts this whole backstory around a different minister, **Pastor Joseph Johnson** (see below), and a private cash sale rather than an auction — the two accounts are not yet reconciled (see Continuity Notes).
_Chapter: 17 (contradicted by draft: Roof)_

**Pastor Joseph ("Joe") Johnson**
Draft-only. The Unitarian pastor who sold the chapel, now running a modest church from a portacabin and funding schools in Africa (his son works at one). Genuinely, almost comically innocent — sold up in the mid-90s amid the elders' millennial/Y2K "the world will end in 2000" fever combined with a decade of failing to fund the painted-roof repairs. Handled Dobby's photo and conceded "it could have been" the French buyer, remembering "that mole." In the current text he occupies the same role the concordance assigns to **Gordon Thorpe** — the two have not been reconciled.
_Draft: Roof_

**Mr Grafton (the younger)**
Draft-only. The Unitarians' solicitor for the chapel sale — a bent lawyer who took "a cut from both ends," waved through D'Aubeilles's all-cash purchase as "perfectly normal," and steered the church toward a dubious investment vehicle. Took over the church's business from his father after the elder Grafton's stroke that spring. The Boht draft has him "disappear not long after." Likely central to laundering the gold.
_Drafts: Roof, Boht_

**Mr Grafton (senior)**
Draft-only. The younger Grafton's father and the church's long-standing solicitor. Would, the pastor believes, have talked the elders out of selling the chapel, but a stroke that spring left the sale in his son's hands.
_Draft: Roof_

**Jennie Bacon**
Draft-only. The church's accountant. Overruled the younger Grafton's suggested investment as "an inappropriate vehicle" and insisted 76.3% of the sale proceeds go into a conventional bank account with all the proper forms filled in.
_Draft: Roof_

**Jonathan Jeffires**
Draft-only (note the spelling — "not Jeffries," per Klee). Author of *Of Mice and Mazes*, a book on robot maze-running "mice" that Klee orders while trying to solve the chapel's bicursal maze. Its oddly-regressive third edition (clunky 1990s home-computer typesetting restored, plus extra professional sections) is itself a clue Klee chews on.
_Draft: Crumbs_

**Anunciata & Donny**
The small Indian couple who run Klee's local café/shop (the one he instantly adopts as his "local"). Anunciata works the counter and helps the clumsy customer pick up a spilled basket of crisps; Donny keeps a sharp eye to make sure he pays.
_Chapter: 15_

**Harrison Corner**
American steel-baron's son from Pennsylvania, subject of *Pissing Blue Steam* – a privately printed volume of (very bad) surrealist poetry found in Boht's library. Toured Europe through WWI (left for Europe the day Franz Ferdinand was assassinated), staying in luxury on both sides of the front. Actually a spy for the Americans who hid his situation assessments inside the appalling poems he left across Paris, Berlin, Constantinople and Vienna. A recent biography by his great-grand-nephew exposed the espionage, turning him into a "code-leaving American James Bond" and sending the book's value skyrocketing – Boht's mistake in having a copy on the shelf.
_Chapter: 16_

## Continuity Notes & Contradictions

_The three untitled draft chapters (Boht, Crumbs, Roof) and Ch 19–21 add material that in places contradicts the settled Ch 1–18 canon. Flagged for reconciliation:_

1. **Who sold the chapel, and how — auction vs private cash sale.** Ch 17 (Mrs Ting): the Unitarians **auctioned** the chapel in the building itself; D'Aubeilles bought it at auction and owned it ~6 months. The Roof draft: there was **no auction** — D'Aubeilles bought it in a **private all-cash deal** (claimed proceeds of a South African farm) laundered through the solicitor Grafton. These cannot both stand.

2. **Which minister — Gordon Thorpe vs Pastor Joseph Johnson.** Ch 17 names the minister who sold up as **Gordon Thorpe** (Unitarian, still alive, "a lovely man," worried the roof would fall mid-"Sing Hosannah"). The Roof draft gives the identical role to **Pastor Joseph Johnson** (portacabin church, Africa schools, Y2K end-times panic). Same function, two different men.

3. **Is the freezer body Dobby, or an impostor?** Ch 13–14 treat the body as Dobby Brand himself. Ch 19/21 and the Boht draft complicate this: a scar where the mole should be, only "a bit" like Dobby — possibly a surgically-altered impostor. Decide whether Dobby is the corpse or the corpse is a decoy.

4. **How much "too much" gold — twice or three times.** Ch 19: "about twice as much gold as there should be." Boht draft: "about three times too much." Pick one multiple.

5. **When was Pausing promoted to inspector.** Ch 14: promoted to inspector "just as I resigned." Ch 19: "they promoted me when they suspended you [Klee]." Different triggering events; timeline needs pinning down.

6. **Pausing's name spelling.** "Yan" (Ch 21) vs "Jan" (Ch 19) — pick one.

7. **Preston Vanz's allegiance.** Framed in Ch 11/14 as Klee's hired security and Pausing's bait-man; the Cubit draft has him and his father Dalton secretly hunting the same gold, blocked by the very security Preston installed. Intentional double-cross, or unresolved? (Also: multiple break-in crews are now in play — Astrid's "bozos," Terpsichore's men, and Dalton's "local morons" — worth tracking that they stay distinct.)

8. **D'Aubeilles ↔ Dobby.** The Roof/Boht drafts hint D'Aubeilles may *be* Dobby (cash, the mole, "could have been"). If pursued, reconcile with the concordance's current treatment of them as separate men and with the freezer-body question above.

9. **The neighbours' nationality.** Established running gag: Mrs Ting thinks the chapel-side neighbours are **German** ("German curries," white wine vinegar). Ch 19/21: they're **Swiss** — it's **Astrid** cooking currywurst and fondue. This one reads as an intended payoff rather than an error, but noting it so the "German" gag stays consistent up to the reveal.
