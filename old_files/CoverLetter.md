What's the purpose of a covering letter exactly? I never knew when I was writing them for job applications. Was it to make it clear why the prospective employer should hire you? Look! I've got exactly the right skills! If they haven't picked that up from the CV, why are they going to pick it up from the covering letter? I always struggled with this subtext: "If you'd actually bothered to read my CV, you'd see that I've got exactly the skills you're asking for."

Or was it more performative? The epistolary equivalent of Yosser Hughes? Gizza job. Please!

Fortunately, I don't have to worry about that anymore. The AI HR assistant figures out I'm over fifty and my CV has been binned before it touches a human eye. 

But what's the point of a covering letter for a literary competition? I have even less of an idea. But I think the subtext is a bit different. I'm guessing you will actually read the submissions, which is why you've cut them off at 5000 words. 

And it is the "blue pencil agency." So, there's an expectation that the grammar and punctuation will be correct. Maybe the purpose of a covering letter for a literary competition is to tell the judges what they're looking at.

So here we go. This is what you're looking at. It's a criminal procedural. It's inspired by a bunch of true crime cases that I've soaked up over the years. I'll own up. I don't yet know how it ends. I don't know who the body is in the freezer. It might be the original Mr Boht. It might be Johnny "The General" Jenkins. Who's he? The mastermind of a gold bullion robbery where only half the gold was ever recovered. And the other half might be hidden in this conceit of an antiques-filled chapeli, adjoining a maze.

It is my attempt at writing a criminal procedural I'd actually like to read. I like the kind of criminal procedurals where I can figure out who did it from the clues. I don't like car chases or fisticuffs. I don't want the protagonist to solve it using clues I can't see. I want the hero or heroine to avenge the wrong that has been wrought with the power of their mind. And I, as a reader, want to have been given a fair chance of doing this too.

Why should I start to write my first novel right now? Well, I recently published a book. It's on a professional subject. And it's non-fiction. It's on the racy subject of project management. The reason I think it got published was I brought something different. A different attitude and a different voice. I managed to find the occasional joke and look at the whole thing in an interesting way. I managed to do that with a subject that makes paint drying look like the winter Olympics.

And maybe that's the final thing you, as judges of a writing competition want from a covering letter. They want an idea of the author's attitude and voice. Maybe that's what employers were looking for in a covering letter. They just wanted the first inkling of an idea of whether this person would be a useful employee, or a pain in the arse. A tribunal waiting to happen.

I really hope you enjoy reading this fragment of my book. 

I'm enjoying writing it.

Yours faithfully,

Mark Stringer.
